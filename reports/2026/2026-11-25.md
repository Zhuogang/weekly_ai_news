# AI Research & Engineering Digest
**November 25, 2026 · Main window: November 22–25 · About 9 minutes**

Three ideas to take into a technical conversation:

- **Teach with information the deployed model will never see.** Future-OPSD uses the actual future during training, then removes it at inference.
- **A smoother reward can teach better research habits.** Mantic mixes resolved outcomes with reference forecasts to reduce noisy RL feedback—and explicitly accepts some teacher bias.
- **A sharper label is not necessarily stronger evidence.** A new text-restoration paper is relevant to your OCR pipeline precisely because it can make inferred text look convincingly real.

## 1. WeMM-Ecom: specialization means defining which product counts as “the same”

*Technical report submitted November 23*

Tencent’s family spans 0.8B dense through 35B-A3B MoE models. It combines progressively more e-commerce-heavy continued pretraining with on-policy distillation and RL, supporting thinking and non-thinking modes.

The interesting contribution for you is the supervision contract: product identity, variant attributes and evidence must line up. One 500 g bag and two 250 g bags can share total quantity while being different SKUs.

On the authors’ 28-benchmark commerce suite, the 9B model scores **72.5 versus 67.3** for size-matched Qwen3.5-9B in thinking mode. The largest model’s headline average hides weaker results on several public commerce tests and spatial reasoning. Twenty-three benchmarks are in-house.

**My take:** a credible candidate for your reranking shortlist, especially exact variant verification. It is not evidence that you should replace a working retrieval system wholesale.

The public repository provides serving examples and links checkpoints across sizes and formats. Check each model’s license separately; the code is Apache 2.0.

Start with the 4B or 9B model on absent-SKU cases, near-identical packaging and pack-count mismatches. Compare automation coverage at your required precision, not merely average matching accuracy.

Sources: [technical report and task definitions](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.30662v1) · [repository and serving instructions](https://github.com/Tencent/WeMM-Ecom) · [model collection](https://huggingface.co/collections/tencent/wemm-ecom)

## Pushing the frontier: better models, better use.

### 2. Mantic’s forecasting agent: learn research behavior with less noisy rewards

*Firsthand engineering post published November 24*

Mantic post-trains Inkling 2 Small using generated forecasting questions and point-in-time research tools. Those tools expose information available at the question’s date, rather than later answers.

The reward mixes the log probability of the realized outcome with expected log score under a frontier-model ensemble’s reference forecast. Intuitively, the reference reduces the randomness of judging a probability distribution from one realized event; outcomes keep learning tied to reality.

**The loop:** generate questions → research and forecast → score → update weights → evaluate held-out forecasts. Humans design data sources, temporal restrictions, rewards and infrastructure. This is persistent post-training, not simply a better prompt.

The authors report matching Fable 5.2 medium at **15× lower cost per forecast**. Their base-model comparisons show improved research behavior, but the post does not provide enough independently reproducible detail to isolate every contribution or establish total training cost.

The explicit tradeoff is bias toward the reference ensemble’s mistakes. Point-in-time tools also do not by themselves eliminate memorized future knowledge in pretrained weights. I did not verify a public task-specific checkpoint.

**Takeaway:** when outcomes are noisy, study whether a lower-variance training signal helps—and evaluate on real outcomes the teacher never labeled.

Source: [Mantic and Thinking Machines: training a scalable forecasting agent](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/training-a-scalable-forecasting-agent/)

### 3. Future-OPSD: let the teacher peek ahead, then train that advantage into the student

*Submitted November 20; late discovery through HF November 23*

The student sees a video’s past and generates its own reasoning and answer. A frozen copy of the initial model scores those tokens twice: with the true future and with an unrelated future.

That contrast helps isolate event-relevant information from generic scene context. Combined with a teacher–student distillation term, it updates the student’s weights. Deployment uses only the past.

For Qwen3-VL-8B, the paper reports **67.8 versus 61.2** on TwiFF’s average score against naive image-privileged self-distillation. A frozen teacher outperforms an EMA teacher in the reported ablation: making the teacher continually change is not automatically helpful.

The cost table uses eight B200s; compared with naive OPSD, the method adds roughly **8–15% training time**. Open-ended evaluation includes model judging, and evidence from one future clip cannot represent every plausible continuation.

The repository exposes training/evaluation code and checkpoint links. It also describes selecting released checkpoints using several evaluation outcomes; distinguish that selection procedure from untouched confirmation.

**RSI verdict:** the student persistently improves, but the teacher is fixed. This is self-distillation, not evidence of recursively accelerating research.

**Try the idea:** use later scan observations as privileged training evidence while preventing future-store or future-date leakage.

Sources: [paper and ablations](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26954v1) · [implementation and checkpoint-selection details](https://github.com/Jingchensun/future-opsd) · [project](https://jingchensun.github.io/future-opsd/)

**What these two methods share:** better supervision can come from a better information position, not just a larger teacher. Neither establishes a loop in which successive models independently improve the process that trains their successors. The practical opportunity is already substantial without making that stronger claim.

## 4. Inkling 2 Mini: fewer stored weights, more computation inside each token

*Released November 20; late discovery, not a new launch today*

Thinking Machines’ open-weight model repeats its middle layers three times per token. It has **26.4B total parameters, 3.4B active parameters**, and a separate **5.4B weight-activation count** accounting for looping.

Those are different quantities. Parameter count alone conceals the repeated work.

The training disclosure is unusually useful: prune two-thirds of experts during midtraining, recover capability using **1T distilled tokens**, then run SFT and RL. Most layers train and deploy natively in NVFP4, with the last four in MXFP8.

The release reports 48.2% on DeepSWE v1.1, but evaluation setups vary. Its comparison chart’s decoding speed is a **theoretical bandwidth ceiling**, not a measured end-to-end serving benchmark.

The model card lists Apache 2.0 weights, an approximately 20 GB mixed-precision checkpoint and supported deployment paths. That footprint is not a promise that every 20 GB device has adequate runtime headroom.

**Why care:** this makes the depth-versus-memory tradeoff concrete. For edge deployment, ask whether looping improves accepted answers per second under your latency budget, including the extra compute—not merely whether the weights fit.

Sources: [architecture, pruning and training disclosure](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/) · [model card, license and deployment requirements](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini)

## 5. Reading the Unreadable: image enhancement can turn a hypothesis into pixels

*Submitted November 11; older paper surfaced on HF November 23*

The paper separates locally readable text from text that requires surrounding context. Its ReasonText benchmark contains **513 image pairs and 2,463 text instances**.

Its training-free method, Reasoning Transfer via Captioning, asks a multimodal model to infer degraded text, then conditions a super-resolution model on that caption. Across six text-conditioned restoration models, results improve.

The evaluation idea is just as useful: crop restored text using ground-truth polygons before recognition, separating restoration quality from detector failures. For DiT4SR, reported overall text-crop accuracy rises from **59.2 to 73.6** with the method.

**The uncomfortable part:** an inferred character can become a clean, plausible character in the enhanced image. The system has not created new optical evidence. The benchmark excludes its unrecoverable category from the main evaluation, so success there does not establish safe behavior when evidence is genuinely insufficient.

Public code and the ReasonText dataset are accessible.

For your pipeline, preserve the original crop and distinguish observed OCR from context-inferred text. A prettier image should never silently become independent confirmation of the model’s own guess.

Sources: [paper and restoration metric](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15449v1) · [code](https://github.com/jasonleex1995/TAISR_needs_Reasoning) · [ReasonText dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Jasonleex1995/ReasonText)

## 6. Embo-Harness: better observations can beat more elaborate instructions

*Submitted November 23*

This robotics study varies action execution, planning, prompts, visual inputs and tool integration around a frontier-model policy.

The useful findings are concrete: end-effector actions work better than joint-space actions; short fixed action chunks help; complementary views and higher resolution matter. Handcrafted plans and failure reminders provide limited benefit in the tested configurations.

The full system reports **83.3% success on RoboLab-120**, versus 57.1% for its strongest reported baseline, and **15 successes in 20 real-world trials**. Controlled interventions suggest accurate spatial target estimation matters more than explicitly generating detailed low-level trajectories.

**What this tells us:** an agent’s apparent reasoning weakness may actually be an observation or interface weakness.

The limitations are substantial: twenty physical trials are a small sample; some concurrent systems have different sensor assumptions; the final integrated result does not isolate every component’s causal contribution. I verified the paper and project page, not a runnable public release.

For your camera workflow, test resolution and viewpoint before adding another page of instructions. Measure the latency cost too. This is an improved external harness around a model, not evidence of updated model weights.

Sources: [paper, controlled studies and full benchmark results](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.31201v1) · [project](https://dravenalg.github.io/projects/Embo-Harness/)

## 7. Protein design: a general model proposes; specialists and the laboratory still decide

*Research account published November 24*

OpenAI reports using a pre-release GPT-6.2 to write code that generates candidate protein shapes. Specialized models then supply sequences and filter candidates; partner Onava synthesizes and tests them.

In the main minibinder experiment, **35 of 322 tested designs showed binding** across five targets. This is experimentally tested activity reported by the collaborators, rather than solely an in-silico score—but it is not independent replication.

The important boundary: “intrinsic design capability” at one stage does not mean an autonomous end-to-end pipeline. The account explicitly says specialist models remain necessary for screening, and complete structure prediction remains a major weakness. Binding also does not establish useful biological function, specificity or therapeutic efficacy.

**My take:** the compelling result is a general-purpose model producing candidates that survive a physical test. The unproven leap would be claiming it replaces specialist biology systems.

This broadens the set of proposal mechanisms scientists can use. It does not explain which proprietary training choices produced the capability, and it is not evidence of recursive model improvement. I did not verify a released reproduction package.

Source: [research account, experimental results and limitations](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-models-demonstrate-intrinsic-protein-design-capabilities/)

2. **[Future-OPSD](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26954v1), alongside its [implementation](https://github.com/Jingchensun/future-opsd).** Read the true-versus-random-future contrast, frozen-teacher ablation and compute table. ## One concrete experiment: does enhanced OCR add evidence—or repeat the model’s guess?

Build a 150-case diagnostic set from hard shelf crops, split across readable text, text needing context, and genuinely missing or occluded identity attributes. Obtain ground truth from a separate high-resolution reference. Keep it hidden from every inference path.

Compare:

- Original image plus your current OCR.
- A VLM reading the original image directly.
- Super-resolution without a text hint.
- VLM-inferred text conditioning super-resolution, followed by OCR.
- The VLM-inferred text supplied directly to the matcher, without generating an enhanced image.

That last condition is essential: it tests whether the image-generation step adds anything beyond carrying forward the original textual hypothesis.

Measure exact attribute accuracy, confident wrong acceptance, abstention, latency and automation coverage at the precision target. Audit cases where the VLM and enhanced-image OCR agree but are both wrong. Also test plausible wrong hints, such as a neighboring flavor or pack size.

**Hypothesis:** enhancement will sometimes help readability, but agreement between its OCR and its conditioning text will overstate independent evidence. If the direct-text condition performs equally well, remove the image-generation stage and keep the uncertainty explicit.

This is a diagnostic experiment, not a recommendation to deploy generative restoration. It answers a concrete question your team can act on: **did we recover information, or render our prior belief more convincingly?**
